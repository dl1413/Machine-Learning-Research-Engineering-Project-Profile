PROMPT 1 — Singular Cardinal Exponentiation under GCH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
Work in $\mathsf{ZFC}$ and assume the Generalized Continuum Hypothesis
($\mathsf{GCH}$). Evaluate the cardinal exponential

$$
\aleph_{\omega}^{\,\aleph_{0}}
$$

and express the answer as a single aleph.

SOLUTION:
Because $\operatorname{cf}(\aleph_{\omega}) = \operatorname{cf}(\omega) = \aleph_0
\le \aleph_0 < \aleph_\omega$, König's theorem forces strict growth:

$$
\aleph_{\omega}^{\,\aleph_0} > \aleph_{\omega}.
$$

Hence $\aleph_{\omega}^{\aleph_0} \ge \aleph_{\omega+1}$. For the upper bound, use the
Hausdorff/GCH computation for a cardinal $\kappa$ with
$\operatorname{cf}(\kappa)\le\aleph_0<\kappa$: under $\mathsf{GCH}$,
$\kappa^{\aleph_0}=\kappa^{+}$. Concretely, cover the sup:

$$
\aleph_{\omega}^{\aleph_0}
= \Big(\sup_{n}\aleph_n\Big)^{\aleph_0}
\le \prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_n^{\aleph_0}
\le \prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_{n+1}
\le \aleph_{\omega}^{\aleph_0},
$$

and under $\mathsf{GCH}$ each $\aleph_n^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_{n+1}$, giving
$\prod_{n<\omega}\aleph_{n+1}=\aleph_{\omega}^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_{\omega+1}$.
Both bounds meet.

ANSWER:
$$
\aleph_{\omega}^{\,\aleph_{0}} = \aleph_{\omega+1}.
$$

TRAP: Answering $\aleph_\omega$ (treating it like a regular cardinal / naive
"$\kappa^{\aleph_0}=\kappa$"), or $2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_1$. König's theorem rules
$\aleph_\omega$ out.

PROMPT 2 — Vaught's "Never Two" Theorem
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
Let $T$ be a complete first-order theory in a countable language with an
infinite model. Let $I(T,\aleph_0)$ denote the number of pairwise
non-isomorphic countable models of $T$. It is a theorem that $I(T,\aleph_0)$
can take any value in $\{1,3,4,5,\dots\}\cup\{\aleph_0,\,2^{\aleph_0}\}$ but is
forbidden from being exactly one particular finite value. State that forbidden
value.

SOLUTION:
Suppose toward contradiction $T$ has exactly two countable models up to
isomorphism. Every complete theory has a countable model realizing only
principal (isolated) types — the prime/atomic model $\mathfrak{A}$ — and, if $T$
is not $\aleph_0$-categorical, a countable model realizing a non-isolated type.
The omitting-types theorem lets one build a countable model omitting any single
non-isolated type. A careful analysis (the Ryll-Nardzewski / Vaught argument)
shows: if $T$ is not $\aleph_0$-categorical it has a prime model and a countably
saturated model, and if these are its only two models one can construct a third,
intermediate model realizing some but not all non-principal types — contradicting
"exactly two." Thus $I(T,\aleph_0)\neq 2$.

ANSWER:
$$
I(T,\aleph_0) \neq 2 \qquad\text{(the forbidden value is } 2\text{).}
$$

TRAP: Guessing $1$ (that is allowed — $\aleph_0$-categoricity) or $3$ (allowed —
the classic $\mathbb{Q}$ with countably many constants "$c_n\to\infty$" example
realizes exactly $3$). Only $2$ is impossible.

PROMPT 3 — Complexity of the Index Set of Total Functions
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
Fix a standard enumeration $(\varphi_e)_{e\in\omega}$ of the partial computable
functions. Consider the index set

$$
\mathrm{Tot} = \{\, e \in \omega : \varphi_e \text{ is total} \,\}.
$$

Determine the exact position of $\mathrm{Tot}$ in the arithmetical hierarchy:
give the lightface pointclass $\Gamma$ for which $\mathrm{Tot}$ is
$\Gamma$-complete.

SOLUTION:
Membership is expressed by
$e\in\mathrm{Tot}\iff \forall x\,\exists s\,[\varphi_{e,s}(x)\!\downarrow]$.
The matrix $\varphi_{e,s}(x)\!\downarrow$ is decidable, the inner $\exists s$ is
$\Sigma^0_1$, and the outer universal quantifier puts the predicate at
$\Pi^0_2$. Hence $\mathrm{Tot}\in\Pi^0_2$.

For hardness, reduce a known $\Pi^0_2$-complete set. Given a $\Pi^0_2$ predicate
$R(n)\equiv\forall x\,\exists y\,S(n,x,y)$ with $S$ decidable, uniformly build a
machine $g_n$ that on input $x$ searches for the least $y$ with $S(n,x,y)$ and
halts. Then $\varphi_{g_n}$ is total $\iff \forall x\,\exists y\,S(n,x,y)\iff
R(n)$, an $m$-reduction $R\le_m \mathrm{Tot}$. Since every $\Pi^0_2$ set reduces
to it, $\mathrm{Tot}$ is $\Pi^0_2$-complete (in particular $\mathrm{Tot}
\equiv_T \emptyset''$).

ANSWER:
$$
\mathrm{Tot} \text{ is } \Pi^0_2\text{-complete}.
$$

TRAP: Answering $\Sigma^0_2$ or $\Pi^0_1$. $\mathrm{Tot}$ is not $\Sigma^0_2$
(it is properly $\Pi^0_2$), and the single "$\forall x\exists s$" alternation
already exceeds $\Pi^0_1$.

PROMPT 4 — Natural (Hessenberg) Sum in Cantor Normal Form
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
Let $\oplus$ denote the natural (Hessenberg) sum of ordinals. Compute

$$
\big(\omega^{\omega} + \omega^{2} + 1\big)\ \oplus\ \big(\omega^{\omega}\cdot 2 + \omega\big)
$$

and write the result in Cantor normal form.

SOLUTION:
The natural sum is computed by writing both ordinals in Cantor normal form as
polynomials in $\omega$ and adding coefficients of equal exponents (regardless of
order), then reassembling in strictly decreasing exponents. Collecting terms by
exponent:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega^{\omega}: &\quad 1 + 2 = 3,\\
\omega^{2}: &\quad 1 + 0 = 1,\\
\omega^{1}: &\quad 0 + 1 = 1,\\
\omega^{0}: &\quad 1 + 0 = 1.
\end{aligned}
$$

ANSWER:
$$
\omega^{\omega}\cdot 3 \;+\; \omega^{2} \;+\; \omega \;+\; 1.
$$

TRAP: Using ordinary (non-commutative) ordinal addition, where the left summand's
lower terms are absorbed:
$(\omega^{\omega}+\omega^2+1)+(\omega^{\omega}\cdot2+\omega)
=\omega^{\omega}\cdot 3+\omega$, dropping the $\omega^2$ and $1$. The natural sum
keeps every term.

PROMPT 5 — Erdős–Rado and the Partition Relation for Pairs
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
Recall the Erdős–Rado theorem: for every $n<\omega$,
$\beth_n^{+}\to(\aleph_1)^{n+1}_{\aleph_0}$, and this is optimal at each level.
Using $n=1$, identify the least cardinal $\kappa$ that Erdős–Rado guarantees to
satisfy the partition relation

$$
\kappa \to (\aleph_1)^{2}_{\aleph_0},
$$

and express $\kappa$ in terms of the continuum $2^{\aleph_0}$.

SOLUTION:
For $n=1$ the theorem reads $\beth_1^{+}\to(\aleph_1)^{2}_{\aleph_0}$, where
$\beth_1 = 2^{\aleph_0}$, so $\beth_1^{+}=(2^{\aleph_0})^{+}$. This value is
optimal: by the Sierpiński coloring there is a partition of the pairs of
$2^{\aleph_0}$ witnessing
$2^{\aleph_0}\not\to(\aleph_1)^{2}_{2}$, hence a fortiori
$2^{\aleph_0}\not\to(\aleph_1)^{2}_{\aleph_0}$. Therefore the continuum itself
fails the relation and the successor is the least guaranteed value.

ANSWER:
$$
\kappa = \beth_1^{+} = \big(2^{\aleph_0}\big)^{+}.
$$

TRAP: Answering $\aleph_1$ or $2^{\aleph_0}$. Sierpiński's coloring shows
$2^{\aleph_0}\not\to(\aleph_1)^2_2$, so nothing at or below the continuum works;
one must pass to $(2^{\aleph_0})^+$.
