PROMPT 1 — Shelah's pcf Bound on the Power of a Singular
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
Work in $\mathsf{ZFC}$. Suppose $\aleph_{\omega}$ is a strong limit cardinal
(equivalently $2^{\aleph_n}<\aleph_\omega$ for all $n<\omega$). Shelah's pcf theory
provides an ABSOLUTE ZFC upper bound of the form $2^{\aleph_{\omega}} < \aleph_{\gamma}$
for a fixed countable ordinal index $\gamma$ that does not depend on the model.
State the sharp index $\gamma$ delivered by Shelah's theorem.

SOLUTION:
The naive expectation "$2^{\aleph_\omega}$ can be anything above $\aleph_\omega$ of
uncountable cofinality" is false for strong-limit singulars of cofinality $\omega$.
Shelah's pcf machinery bounds the pseudopowers: for the interval
$\{\aleph_n : n<\omega\}$ one has
$\operatorname{pcf}(\{\aleph_n : n<\omega\})$ has cardinality $<\aleph_{\omega_4}$,
and since $\aleph_\omega$ strong limit gives
$2^{\aleph_\omega}=\aleph_\omega^{\aleph_0}=\max\operatorname{pcf}(\{\aleph_n\})$,
one obtains

$$\aleph_{\omega} \text{ strong limit} \ \Longrightarrow\ 2^{\aleph_{\omega}} < \aleph_{\omega_4}.$$

ANSWER:
$$\gamma = \omega_4, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad 2^{\aleph_{\omega}} < \aleph_{\omega_4}.$$

TRAP: Answering $\omega_1$ (only the cofinality constraint
$\operatorname{cf}(2^{\aleph_\omega})>\aleph_0$, from König) or "$2^{\aleph_\omega}$
is unbounded." The whole surprise of pcf theory is the finite index $4$ on
$\omega$; $\aleph_{\omega_4}$ is the sharp published bound.

PROMPT 2 — Kunen's Inconsistency: the Sharp Rank Level
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
Let $j : V \to M$ be a nontrivial elementary embedding with critical point
$\kappa_0$, and let $\lambda = \sup_{n<\omega}\kappa_n$ be the supremum of the
critical sequence $\kappa_{n+1}=j(\kappa_n)$. The rank-into-rank axioms assert the
existence of a nontrivial elementary embedding $j : V_{\lambda+m}\to V_{\lambda+m}$.
Kunen's theorem proves that such an embedding is INCONSISTENT with $\mathsf{ZFC}$
starting at a least value of $m$. State that least $m$.

SOLUTION:
The embeddings $j:V_\lambda\to V_\lambda$ (axiom I3) and $j:V_{\lambda+1}\to
V_{\lambda+1}$ (axiom I1) are not refuted by Kunen's argument and are studied as
strong large-cardinal hypotheses. Kunen's proof — via a Ramsey-type
$\omega$-Jónsson function / the existence of an $\omega$-sequence
$\langle \lambda_n\rangle$ moved by $j$ contradicting a combinatorial partition
(Solovay's stationary splitting / the Erdős–Hajnal function on
$[\lambda]^{\omega}$) — uses a subset of $V_{\lambda+2}$ (a function on
$[\lambda]^\omega$). Hence it rules out $j:V_{\lambda+2}\to V_{\lambda+2}$:

$$\nexists\ \text{nontrivial elementary } j : V_{\lambda+2} \to V_{\lambda+2}.$$

ANSWER:
$$m = 2.$$

TRAP: Answering $m=1$ (that is axiom I1, consistent as far as is known) or $m=0$
(axiom I3). The inconsistency first bites at rank $\lambda+2$, because the killing
combinatorial object lives one power set above $V_{\lambda+1}$.

PROMPT 3 — Proof-Theoretic Ordinal of ATR_0
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
In the reverse-mathematics / ordinal-analysis setting, let $|T|_{\mathrm{PTO}}$
denote the proof-theoretic (supremum-of-provable-well-orderings) ordinal of a
theory $T$. It is classical that $|\mathsf{PA}|_{\mathrm{PTO}} = \varepsilon_0$ and
$|\mathsf{ACA}_0|_{\mathrm{PTO}} = \varepsilon_0$. Determine
$|\mathsf{ATR}_0|_{\mathrm{PTO}}$, the proof-theoretic ordinal of
$\mathsf{ATR}_0$ (arithmetical transfinite recursion).

SOLUTION:
$\mathsf{ATR}_0$ calibrates predicative provability: it proves transfinite recursion
of arithmetical operators along any well-ordering. Its proof-theoretic ordinal is
the Feferman–Schütte ordinal $\Gamma_0$ — the least ordinal $\alpha$ closed under
the Veblen function, i.e. $\Gamma_0 = \varphi_{\Gamma_0}(0)$, the least fixed point
of $\xi\mapsto\varphi_\xi(0)$. This is precisely the ordinal marking the limit of
predicativity (Feferman–Schütte analysis).

ANSWER:
$$|\mathsf{ATR}_0|_{\mathrm{PTO}} = \Gamma_0 = \varphi_{\Gamma_0}(0).$$

TRAP: Answering $\varepsilon_0$ (that is $\mathsf{PA}$ / $\mathsf{ACA}_0$, one Big
Five level below) or the Bachmann–Howard ordinal
$\psi_\Omega(\varepsilon_{\Omega+1})$ (that is $\mathsf{ID}_1$; and
$\Pi^1_1\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_0$ is even larger). $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ sits exactly at
$\Gamma_0$.

PROMPT 4 — Martin Axiom Collapses Cichoń's Diagram
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
Assume $\mathsf{MA} + \neg\mathsf{CH}$ (Martin's Axiom with the negation of the
Continuum Hypothesis). Consider Cichoń's diagram of cardinal characteristics of the
continuum: $\operatorname{add}(\mathcal N)$, $\operatorname{add}(\mathcal M)$,
$\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal N)$, $\operatorname{cov}(\mathcal M)$, $\mathfrak b$,
$\mathfrak d$, $\operatorname{non}(\mathcal N)$, $\operatorname{non}(\mathcal M)$,
$\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal M)$, $\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal N)$. Give the common
value that ALL ten of these characteristics take under $\mathsf{MA}+\neg\mathsf{CH}$.

SOLUTION:
$\mathsf{MA}$ implies that the union of fewer than $\mathfrak c$ meager (resp. null)
sets is meager (resp. null), which forces the bottom of the diagram up:
$\operatorname{add}(\mathcal N)=\mathfrak c$. Since $\operatorname{add}(\mathcal N)$
is the provable minimum and $\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal N)$ the provable maximum of
the diagram, and both equal $\mathfrak c$, every entry is squeezed:

$$\operatorname{add}(\mathcal N)=\cdots=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal N)=\mathfrak c = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

ANSWER:
$$\text{Every characteristic } = \mathfrak c = 2^{\aleph_0}.$$

TRAP: Answering $\aleph_1$ (that is what $\neg\mathsf{CH}$ makes them NOT — under
$\mathsf{CH}$ everything is trivially $\aleph_1=\mathfrak c$, but with $\neg$CH the
content is that $\mathsf{MA}$ still pins them all to $\mathfrak c>\aleph_1$), or
naming a proper subset that collapses.

PROMPT 5 — Martin–Steel: Woodin Cardinals and Projective Determinacy
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROMPT:
By the Martin–Steel theorem, the existence of $n$ Woodin cardinals with a
measurable cardinal above them all implies the determinacy of a specific level of
the projective hierarchy. For a given $n\ge 1$, state the exact projective
pointclass $\Pi^1_{k}$ whose determinacy is proved, i.e. give $k$ as a function of
$n$.

SOLUTION:
Martin and Steel (building on the tree/iterability analysis of homogeneously Suslin
sets) proved: if there exist $n$ Woodin cardinals with a measurable above, then
every $\boldsymbol\Pi^1_{n+1}$ set of reals is determined. Equivalently $n$ Woodins
$\Rightarrow \boldsymbol\Pi^1_{n+1}$-determinacy; letting $n\to\infty$ (infinitely
many Woodins) yields full Projective Determinacy. The base case $n=1$ gives
$\boldsymbol\Pi^1_2$-determinacy from one Woodin (plus a measurable above).

ANSWER:
$$k = n+1, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad n \text{ Woodins} \ \Rightarrow\ \boldsymbol\Pi^1_{n+1}\text{-determinacy}.$$

TRAP: Answering $k=n$ (off by one — one Woodin already gives $\Pi^1_2$, not
$\Pi^1_1$; $\Pi^1_1$-determinacy needs only a measurable, by Martin) or $k=2n$.
